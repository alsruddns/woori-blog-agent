# Evidence-grounded editorial writing standard

Generated articles should read as reader-centered reported explainers or expert columns, not press-release summaries or internal assessment reports. Lead with the reader's central question and an evidence-based point. Build context and analysis around the article's actual subject; use topic-specific headings, focused paragraphs, varied sentence structure, and a concise ending with concrete unresolved questions.

### Analysis depth and search usefulness

When an article makes an analytical point, explain the chain **verified fact → meaning → reader implication**. This is a reasoning aid, not a mandatory paragraph template: use it where it clarifies the issue, and do not force every paragraph into the same pattern. Interpretations must be reasonably derived from reviewed evidence and must be signaled as analysis. Do not add unsupported forecasts, expert statements, statistics, interviews, or firsthand reporting.

Answer the likely reader question early and make the article's scope clear. Search terms may inform the topic and metadata, but never justify keyword repetition, inflated length, misleading titles, or invented search-volume and traffic claims. Prefer useful detail, readable structure, and accurate wording over a word-count target. Rendering and CSS may organize only content the author supplied; they must not create or imply new editorial claims.

Keep sourced fact, attributed position, and editorial analysis distinct. Explain implications only when they follow logically from reviewed evidence, and signal analysis as analysis. Preserve the difference between proposed, planned, approved, enacted, and paid. Do not invent expert opinions, interviews, statistics, firsthand experience, or forecasts.

Write for the target language's readers rather than translating sentence by sentence. Explain unfamiliar institutions, policy terms, units, and procedures in natural local language while preserving the evidence's exact meaning. Cite factual paragraphs. Paraphrase independently and avoid unnecessary quotation, repeated caveats, filler, keyword repetition, and internal workflow vocabulary such as package, quality gate, or machine validation. Keep SEO titles, search descriptions, labels, and other publication metadata distinct from article body content.

The deterministic quality gate checks structure and traceability; it cannot certify semantic accuracy, analytical merit, rights, or naturalness. Failed checks remain held, and every article requires human editorial review before publication readiness. `generation_ready` and `publish_ready` are independent of a stylistic improvement.
